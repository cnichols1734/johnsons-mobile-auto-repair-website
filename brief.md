### Design Direction

- **Industry:** Mobile Auto Repair / Mechanic — trustworthy, responsive, practical
- **Tone:** Reliable, convenient, always-ready. This is the mechanic who shows up when you need them. NOT a corporate chain, NOT a high-end service—blue-collar, hardworking, and local.
- **Typography-led design.** Use a strong, bold sans-serif for headings (Outfit, Sora) paired with a highly readable body font. The type should convey competence and reliability.
- **Color palette:** Deep navy or charcoal (#1A1A2E) as base, clean white (#FFFFFF) backgrounds, with a bold safety-orange or red accent (#E63946) reminiscent of automotive emergency colors. High contrast for visibility.
- **Photography:** Use Unsplash images (search: "mechanic working," "mobile mechanic," "car repair roadside," "auto diagnostic"). A dark overlay on hero images for text legibility.

---

### CRITICAL — DO NOT DO THESE THINGS

These are the patterns that make AI-generated sites look amateur. Avoid every single one:

1. **No translateY hover float effects on cards.** Real sites don't make every element levitate on hover. Use subtle border-color changes or opacity shifts only on genuinely interactive elements (buttons, links).

2. **No icon-in-a-rounded-square as the repeated visual motif.** Do not put Font Awesome icons inside 48-52px colored/bordered squares and repeat that pattern in every section. If you use icons at all, use them inline with text or as small accents — not as the centerpiece of every card.

3. **No identical section structure repeated.** Do NOT use the same "pill tag → section title → subtitle → card grid" skeleton for every section. Each section should have its own distinct layout. Mix full-width sections, asymmetric two-column layouts, centered single-column text, large pull quotes, etc.

4. **No colored accent cards to indicate "featured" items.** Don't make one card dark navy and another card red to show importance. Use size, position, and typography weight to create hierarchy instead.

5. **No spinning SVG loader or themed loading animation.** Use a clean, minimal loading animation — a thin progress bar or a simple fade-in is more professional than a custom SVG animation.

6. **No "scroll mouse" indicator at the bottom of the hero.** This is an AI cliché. Remove it entirely.

7. **No generic Font Awesome icons as primary visual interest.** Icons like `fa-wrench`, `fa-car`, `fa-bolt` in colored boxes are the #1 tell of AI-generated sites. Use typography, photography, and layout instead.

8. **No frosted-glass / backdrop-filter cards floating over gradient backgrounds.** This is the most overused AI pattern. Use solid backgrounds, clean edges, and real content hierarchy.

9. **No letter-spacing: 1-2px uppercase tiny pill labels on every section.** One or two is fine. Every section having an identical "WHAT WE DO" / "ABOUT US" / "TESTIMONIALS" pill tag is a pattern.

10. **No emergency floating call button with a pulsing box-shadow.** Just make the phone number prominent in the nav and hero. A sticky bottom bar on mobile is fine but should be a clean, simple bar — not a floating circle with a glow.

### DESIGN PRINCIPLES — DO THESE INSTEAD

**Let typography carry the design.** Use a bold, distinctive display font for headings (Outfit, Sora — bold weights) paired with a clean readable body font. Use dramatic size contrast: 56-72px hero headings paired with 16-17px body text. Use font weight variation (700-800 for headlines, 400-500 for body).

**Use a real hero image.** Source from Unsplash. A dark overlay (`linear-gradient(rgba(0,0,0,0.5), rgba(0,0,0,0.7))`) over a real photo of a mechanic at work, diagnostic equipment, or a roadside repair scene instantly grounds the site.

**Give every section a different layout:**
- **Hero:** Full-viewport image background with overlay, large headline ("Mobile Auto Repair in Old River-Winfree"), subheadline about convenience, prominent phone CTA, and key benefits row ("We Come to You", "Same-Day Service", "No Towing Needed")
- **Services:** Clean multi-column list grouped by service type — Diagnostics, Repairs, Maintenance, Emergency
- **About:** Asymmetric two-column with the mobile service story on one side and a service area map on the other
- **How It Works:** Step-by-step process: 1. Call/Text, 2. Schedule, 3. We Come to You, 4. Get Back on the Road
- **Service Area:** Map showing Old River-Winfree, Cove, and surrounding Chambers County coverage
- **Contact:** Simple form for appointment requests + prominent phone number

**Whitespace is your primary design tool.** Sections should have generous padding (100-120px vertical on desktop). Content should never feel cramped. Let things breathe.

**Limit your color usage.** Use your accent color for CTAs and 1-2 emphasis moments only. Everything else should be your neutral palette (dark text on light backgrounds, light text on dark hero).

**Mobile is the priority.** Most visitors will be on phones, possibly stranded or searching for immediate help. The phone number should be immediately tappable.

**Testimonials should feel real and important.** Use large typographic quote marks (big `"` character), generous spacing, and let the words speak. Don't shrink into tiny cards.

**The loading animation should be nearly invisible.** A thin bar across the top of the screen that fills and fades, or a simple full-page fade-in. Nothing themed, nothing clever.
